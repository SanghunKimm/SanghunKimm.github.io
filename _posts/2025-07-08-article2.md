### Problem 3.2.21

Let $A$ be an $m \times n$ matrix with rank rank $m$. 
Prove that there exists an $n \times m $ matrix $B$ such that $AB = I_m$.

### Solution 

Let $$A$$ be an $$m \times n$$ matrix with $$\mathrm{rank}(A) = m$$.  
We aim to prove that there exists an $$n \times m$$ matrix $$B$$ such that:

$$
AB = I_m.
$$

Let the columns of $$B$$ be denoted by $$b_j$$. Then:

$$
AB = I_m \Longleftrightarrow A b_j = e_j \quad \text{for all } j = 1, \ldots, m.
$$

Without loss of generality, fix $$j = 1$$.  
Let $$A'$$ be the $$(m - 1) \times n$$ matrix obtained by removing the first row of $$A$$.  
Then:

$$
A' b_1 = \mathbf{0} \quad \Rightarrow \quad b_1 \in N(A').
$$

Note that:

$$
\mathrm{nullity}(A') = n - (m - 1) = n - m + 1 \geq 1,
$$

while:

$$
\mathrm{nullity}(A) = n - m.
$$

Since we want $$A b_1 = e_1$$, it suffices to show that $$b_1 \notin N(A)$$.  
This is easily verified because:

$$
N(A) \leq N(A') \quad \text{and} \quad \dim N(A) = n - m < \dim N(A') = n - m + 1.
$$

Therefore, there exists $$b_1 \in N(A') \setminus N(A)$$ such that:

$$
A' b_1 = \mathbf{0}, \quad \text{but} \quad A b_1 = e_1.
$$

By scalar multiplication and the same reasoning for each $$j$$, we can construct column vectors $$b_j$$ satisfying $$A b_j = e_j$$.  
Thus, we obtain a matrix $$B$$ such that:

$$
AB = I_m. \quad \blacksquare
